Operations and financial plan

How the system works, what it costs, what to charge, how to save on routes, which machines to buy and when it starts making money. Málaga, November 2026.

2. How the system works (operations)

2.1 Order flow

 Customer orders (web/WhatsApp)  →  Awaiting pickup
        ↓ rider collects in a 2 h slot, weighs the bag in front of the customer
 Collected (on the bike)  →  At the hub (weighed, sorted, QR-labelled, scent noted)
        ↓ laundry staff assign it to a free washer (app /hub)
 Washing (45 min)  →  Drying (40 min)  →  Ironing  →  Vacuum packing  →  Ready
        ↓ the optimiser groups ready deliveries by zone and assigns them to a rider
 Out for delivery  →  Delivered (photo + confirmation in the rider app)

Every status is logged with time and person in the app, so at any moment you know what is being washed, what is sitting idle waiting for a machine, what is ready and where it has to be delivered, and the customer can receive an automatic WhatsApp at each step.

2.2 Where the washing happens: at the staff's homes or in a hub?

Your starting idea is that several local women run the washing machines and do the packing. There are two ways to do this and it is worth deciding before buying anything:

A. Home network (each person washes at home) B. Micro-hub (premises with machines, staff work in shifts)
Investment Minimal (bags, small vacuum sealers, scents) €25,000-45,000 in machines and installation
Capacity Domestic 8 kg washer, cycle + drying ≈ 2.5-3 h → 2-3 orders/day per person Professional 11-20 kg washer, 45 min cycle → 8-10 orders/day per machine
Energy cost Domestic dryer: very inefficient, and the worker pays for it (legal and motivation problem) Professional dryers, industrial tariff
Quality and traceability Hard to standardise (household smells, pets, customers' loads mixed up) Uniform process, scent control, scales, labels
Vacuum packing and ironing Slow domestic sealer; domestic iron Chamber vacuum sealer, steam ironing table with boiler (3x faster)
Legal Home-based work = employment relationship. Paying someone "per load" without a contract is a bogus self-employed arrangement: fine + back contributions. Normal part-time contracts (dry-cleaning and laundry collective agreement)
Routes The rider has to visit N homes on top of the customers: more km A single loading and unloading point

Recommendation: micro-hub (B), with the same women as part-time employees. It is more investment, but it is the only way to offer uniform ironing and vacuum packing and to scale to 30-50 orders/day. The home network (A) only makes sense as a 1-2 month proof of concept with 10-15 trusted customers to validate demand before signing a lease; even then the people must be registered as employees, however few hours they work.

2.3 The hub

  • Premises of 100-120 m² with three-phase power (or the option to install it), drains and an air outlet for the dryers. Ideal location: Perchel / Cruz de Humilladero / around María Zambrano station: 10-15 minutes by bike from every Zone 1 neighbourhood and rents are lower than in the Centro. Alternative: the San Rafael or Guadalhorce industrial estates (cheaper, but 20-25 min by bike to the Centro). Indicative rent: €900-1,500/month + VAT (2025-26 listings on Fotocasa / CompanySpace).
  • Areas: reception and weighing · 3 washers · 3 dryers · 2 ironing stations · folding and packing table · shelving for ready orders sorted by route.
  • Shifts: 7:00-15:00 and 15:00-21:00. Afternoon pickups are washed first thing the next morning; morning pickups are delivered in the afternoon (24 h) or the next day (48 h).

2.4 Bike delivery

  • 2 electric cargo-bikes with a box (capacity 80-100 kg / 4-6 bags of 10 kg). Málaga city is flat along the whole coastal strip; only Ciudad Jardín and Teatinos have gentle hills.
  • Each rider makes ≈ 7 stops/hour in dense areas (4 min per stop + 3-4 min pedalling). With 7 hours on the road: ≈ 50 stops/day per rider = 25 orders (each order is one pickup + one delivery). Capacity of the pair: ≈ 50 orders/day. Above that, a third rider is needed.
  • The rider's phone sends its GPS position to the app every 5 s: you see it on the map and the customer can get a "your rider is 5 minutes away" message.

2.5 Scents and vacuum packing (the differentiator)

  • 6 scents: sea breeze, lavender, clean cotton, Mediterranean citrus, Málaga jasmine and unscented (hypoallergenic). Applied with professional fabric perfume in the final rinse (≈ €0.03/kg) and a final spray before packing. Loads must be washed by scent, or washers with automatic dosing used.
  • Vacuum packing: 60-70 litre PA/PE bags (€0.50-0.70 each), 2 per 8 kg order. A chamber vacuum sealer (€1,500-3,500) takes 30-40 s per bag; external sealers (€300-600) work but take 2-3 min and the embossed bags cost twice as much. Vacuum compresses the clothes by ≈ 60 %: more orders fit in the bike box and the customer perceives "new clothes".
  • Careful with ironing: a fully compressed vacuum pack creases ironed clothes. Use a partial vacuum (leave 20-30 % of the air in) and fold with card or tissue paper between shirts. Test it on your own clothes before selling it.

Key takeaways:

  1. Hub labour rules everything. €14.40 of every order is laundry staff hours. Every 5 minutes shaved off the process (standardised folding, boiler iron, chamber vacuum sealer) saves ≈ €1/order = €660/month at 30 orders/day. That is why a home network with slow machines does not add up.
  2. Riders are an almost fixed cost. At 15 orders/day they are 31 % utilised: starting with 1 full-time rider + 1 part-time saves €950/month until you pass 25 orders/day.
  3. The kg mix matters little for cost, a lot for revenue. Washing 15 kg costs only €2-3 more than washing 8 kg (almost the same labour), but it brings in €13 more. Push the L bag and bed-linen packs.

5.3 Break-even

  • Contribution per order (net revenue − variable labour − consumables) ≈ €16.70.
  • Monthly fixed costs (riders + premises + the rest) ≈ €6,527.
  • Break-even ≈ 391 orders/month ≈ 18 orders/day.
  • With 1.5 riders instead of 2: ≈ 334 orders/month ≈ 15 orders/day.

A realistic target: 10-15 orders/day in month 3, 25-30 in months 6-9, 50 in year 2 with a third rider and one more shift at the hub.

6. How we save on routes

6.1 Golden rules

  1. Zone by day. Zone 1 West (Huelin, Carretera de Cádiz, Cruz de Humilladero) Mondays and Thursdays; Centro/Soho/Malagueta every day (that is where the density is); Pedregalejo/El Palo Tuesdays and Fridays; Teatinos/Ciudad Jardín Wednesdays and Saturdays. The customer picks a day among those available for their neighbourhood. This multiplies stop density per route, which is what decides delivery cost.
  2. 2-hour slots, never "whenever you like". It lets you chain 10-14 stops per slot.
  3. Pickup and delivery in the same visit for subscribers: the rider drops the clean bag and takes the dirty one. One stop instead of two: it cuts delivery cost per order from €5.76 to €2.88.
  4. The hub as the single origin. The rider leaves loaded with the zone's deliveries, picks up along the same streets and comes back with the collections. The app orders the stops with a nearest-neighbour algorithm improved with 2-opt (on a 15-20 stop route it saves 15-30 % of km versus the order in which orders arrived).
  5. Weight in the box. With vacuum, 6 M bags fit in a cargo-bike box. Without it, 3-4.

7.3 How to make it cheaper

  • Minimum start (≈ €20,000-25,000): 2 second-hand washers of 10-13 kg (≈ €1,800-2,500), 2 second-hand dryers (≈ €1,200-1,500), external vacuum sealer, 1 new cargo-bike + 1 regular e-bike with a trailer, and grow from cash flow. Risk: breakdowns and less capacity.
  • Machinery renting / leasing: Girbau, Primer and Fagor dealers offer 60-month renting at ≈ 1.8-2.2 % of the value per month (€30,000 → €550-650/month) with maintenance included. It turns €30,000 of capex into a fixed cost that enters the model with little impact (we already count €357/month of depreciation).
  • Heat-pump dryers: cost ≈ €1,000 more each but use 40-50 % less energy and need no air outlet. At 30 orders/day they save ≈ €250/month in electricity: they pay for themselves in a year.
  • Gas instead of electricity for the dryers: cheaper to run, but needs a gas installation and inspections. Only if the premises already have gas.
  • Grants: Andalucía Emprende and the ICO/ENISA lines for young entrepreneurs; Málaga City Council (Promálaga) has start-up grants. Book an appointment before buying.

8. Risks and how we cover them

Risk Mitigation
Damaged or lost garments Photo of every bag on receipt, QR label per bag, liability insurance covering garments (≈ €100/month), clear terms (we do not wash anything labelled "dry clean only").
Laundry staff: employment relationship Part-time contracts from day one, Málaga dry-cleaning and laundry collective agreement, occupational risk prevention. Paying "per load" without a contract is the fast track to an inspection.
Ironing eating the margin Basic ironing as standard (only what needs it), full ironing always as a paid extra, boiler tables, time the work and post times per staff member in the hub.
Low order density at the start Zone by day, do not open Zone 2 until Zone 1 has 15 orders/day, start with 1.5 riders.
Price competition (Mr Jeff) Do not compete on basic washing. Always communicate "ironed, vacuum packed and scented".
Vacuum creasing ironed clothes Partial vacuum, tests before launch, card between shirts.
Activity licence Laundry activity (IAE 971.1) usually requires a licence with a technical project, soundproofing and ventilation. Confirm with the City Council before signing a lease; avoid premises under homes if possible.

9. Next steps (90 days)

  1. Weeks 1-2: Validate demand: landing page (/pedido) + Instagram + 200 flyers in Soho/Malagueta; target 30 pre-bookings. Wash 10 orders from friends "by hand" to time the real process and test the vacuum with ironed clothes.
  2. Weeks 3-4: Request 3 machinery quotes (Girbau Málaga, Primer dealer, Fagor Industrial), new and renting, and visit 5 premises in Perchel / Cruz de Humilladero. Check the licence.
  3. Month 2: Incorporate an SL (€3,000 share capital), register under IAE 971.1, liability insurance, hire 2 part-time laundry staff and 1 rider; buy/rent machinery; install.
  4. Month 3: Launch in Zone 1 targeting 10 orders/day; sign 3-5 holiday-rental managers to fill the machines in the morning.
  5. Months 4-6: Second rider, subscriptions, open Zone 2, get hub times below 50 min.
